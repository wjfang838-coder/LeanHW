Module and integration contract

Module: freq_divbyodd.
Ports below are in exact declaration order:
- clk: input 1 bit.
- rst_n: input 1 bit.
- clk_div: output 1 bit.

Public parameters (preserve names, defaults and symbolic port ranges):
-  NUM_DIV: default = 5.

Event convention: each table row drives the explicitly named clock levels and data, then observes settled outputs. The model must apply the edge described by those levels exactly once; it must not insert a second pipeline delay.

1. Module and ports



3. Reset

Reset `rst_n` is active low and asynchronous. Whenever `rst_n` is low, `clk_div` is immediately `1`, independently of `clk`, and remains `1` throughout the reset interval. Reset assertion has priority over a simultaneous clock edge. Reset also restarts the divider cadence at its initial phase.

Before reset has ever been asserted, `clk_div` is unknown at power-up and through the first rising edge. It becomes `0` after the first falling edge and remains `0` under continued clocking until reset is asserted. The implementation must leave this power-up value uninitialized: no initial block or declaration initializer may define it.

When `rst_n` is released after having been asserted, `clk_div` remains at the reset-established value `1`; release alone neither changes the output nor advances the cadence. The first later clock edge is the first advancing edge of the restarted cadence. Thus, on the first step after release, the output remains `1`; if that step contains a clock edge, that edge advances the internal cadence and its effect is observable only in the following sampled row.

The reset state consists of `clk_div = 1`, the divider at the beginning of its initial phase, and the previously observed clock level needed to recognize the next real edge. Before the first reset assertion, result validity is independent of the stored bit value; after reset, `clk_div` is valid and retains its last level between edges.

4. Timing

`clk_div` is a registered periodic output advanced by both rising and falling edges of `clk`. A steady `clk` level causes no advancement. Assertion of `rst_n` affects it combinationally and immediately; deassertion has no immediate output effect.

For the concrete `NUM_DIV = 5` instance, after reset release `clk_div` remains high through the third rising edge, falls after the third falling edge, remains low until it rises after the sixth rising edge, and then alternates every five half-periods. In steady operation its period is ten half-periods, equal to five complete `clk` cycles. Each high interval and each low interval lasts five half-periods, so the two level durations differ in their distribution across rising and falling edges but contain equal half-period counts.

For any supported odd `NUM_DIV`, the reset-established initial high interval continues through `(NUM_DIV + 1) / 2` rising edges and ends after `(NUM_DIV + 1) / 2` falling edges. Steady-state transitions are then `NUM_DIV` half-periods apart, so one full `clk_div` period is `2 * NUM_DIV` half-periods, equal to `NUM_DIV` input-clock cycles. Each steady-state high interval and each steady-state low interval lasts exactly `NUM_DIV` half-periods.

Trace row `k` is an observation after settling at the interface-defined half-cycle step, sampled before that row's pending transition is committed. Therefore a clock edge presented in row `k` first affects the registered output in row `k+1`. This observation convention describes the trace and does not replace the circuit's edge-counted period and delay laws above.

5. Behaviour

Only changes of `clk` level are clock edges. With `rst_n` high after reset, successive rising and falling clk edges advance a continuous divide-by-five waveform whose clk_div transitions alternate between falling and rising clk edges five half-periods apart; a steady clk level has no effect, clocks while rst_n is low leave clk_div high, reset assertion takes priority over a simultaneous clk edge, and uninterrupted or repeated clocking continues the cadence until reset restarts it.

Driving rst_n low immediately forces clk_div to 1'b1 regardless of clk and overrides a simultaneous clk edge, holding it high despite further clocking; driving rst_n high causes no immediate output change, the divide-by-five cadence resumes from its initial phase on later clock edges, a new assertion interrupts and restarts that cadence, and repeated reset intervals behave identically.

Assertion of rst_n low makes clk_div high immediately and release retains that level; because each table row is sampled before its active edge, an edge's effect is first observable after that edge, with the post-reset output remaining high through the third rising edge, falling after the third falling edge, remaining low until it rises after the sixth rising edge, then alternating every five half-periods until another reset immediately restores and holds the high level before a fresh restart.

For the public parameter `NUM_DIV`, the complete steady-state law is the same with each occurrence of five half-periods replaced by `NUM_DIV` half-periods. `NUM_DIV` is restricted to odd positive values, as this module's contract is division by an odd number. The default value is `5`. Reset and pre-reset behavior do not depend on the parameter.

Every scalar input value is interpreted by its single hardware bit. Thus `rst_n = 0` asserts reset and `rst_n = 1` releases it; `clk = 0` and `clk = 1` are levels, and only a level change is an edge. There are no additional input encodings.

## Normative clarification after closure validation

The cadence is defined by actual changes of clk, with a 0-to-1 change being a rising edge and a 1-to-0 change being a falling edge. While rst_n is low, every such edge is ignored and clk_div is held at 1. Assertion of rst_n low takes effect immediately and has priority over any coincident clk edge; it also discards all prior cadence history. Deassertion alone is not an edge and does not advance the cadence. The clk level most recently presented while reset was asserted is retained solely so that the first subsequent change of clk is recognized with its correct edge polarity.

After a reset assertion, the initial high phase counts falling edges only. Its terminating edge is the ((NUM_DIV + 1) / 2)th falling edge after release, including a falling edge on the first post-release step when clk changes from the level last presented during reset. Rising edges during this initial phase do not contribute to that count. On the terminating falling edge, clk_div changes from 1 to 0. Thereafter, every rising and every falling edge contributes exactly one half-period to the cadence. The edge on which clk_div changes is the final, included edge of the current NUM_DIV-edge interval; the next interval begins with the following clk edge. Consequently, successive changes of clk_div are exactly NUM_DIV clk edges apart, without an additional edge before or after reloading the interval.

For row-based observations, inputs and the settled asynchronous-reset effect are observed first, before the row's clk-edge state transition is committed. Thus a clk_div change caused by an edge presented in one row is first visible in the next row. This one-row observation latency must not be added to the edge counts above: for NUM_DIV = 5, the third post-release falling edge causes the initial 1-to-0 change, and each fifth clk edge thereafter causes the next change.

Before rst_n has ever been low, clk_div is unknown through the first rising edge and through the first falling-edge row under this pre-transition observation rule. That first falling edge establishes clk_div as 0, visible beginning with the following row; continued pre-reset clocking leaves it at 0. Once reset has been asserted, clk_div is always known and retains its last defined value between edges.
