1. Module and ports.



3. Reset.

There is no reset input, reset operation, clock, or stored state. At power-up, including before any edge could have occurred, out has the same fully determined combinational value as at every other time: out is 1'b1 exactly when a and b are both 1'b0, and is 1'b0 otherwise. There is consequently no first reset edge and no first post-release step.

4. Timing.

out is combinational and continuously reflects the current values of a and b. Its latency from either input is zero clock cycles. It is neither a pulse nor a periodic signal.

The evaluated trace uses observation steps: row k denotes the settled combinational observation for the row-k stimulus, with successive rows separated by the interface step interval if one is supplied. This observation convention does not imply a 10 ns clock tick, a circuit clock period, or a propagation-delay law. This circuit has no clock or specified numerical period or delay.

5. Behaviour.

out is the one-bit NOR of a and b, so it is 1'b1 exactly when both inputs are 1'b0 and is 1'b0 otherwise.

At all times, a=1'b1 forces out to 1'b0, while a=1'b0 makes out equal to the complement of b; every change of a, including a change simultaneous with b or any repeated change, is reflected combinationally with no modes, capture, interruption, or retained transaction state.

At all times, b=1'b1 forces out to 1'b0, while b=1'b0 makes out equal to the complement of a; every change of b, including a change simultaneous with a or any repeated change, is reflected combinationally with no modes, capture, interruption, or retained transaction state.

out continuously equals ~(a | b), with no reset, clock, capture or observation edge, initialization interval, hold or clear interval, completion, or restart; therefore a row sampled before any edge observes the combinational value for the inputs at that sampling time and there is no edge-counted latency.

Thus the complete input function is: a=1'b0 and b=1'b0 gives out=1'b1; a=1'b0 and b=1'b1 gives out=1'b0; a=1'b1 and b=1'b0 gives out=1'b0; and a=1'b1 and b=1'b1 gives out=1'b0. These are all possible values of the one-bit inputs.
