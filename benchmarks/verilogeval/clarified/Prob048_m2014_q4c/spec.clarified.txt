Event convention: row k drives input i[k], waits for combinational settling and observes out(s[k], i[k]) BEFORE the active clock edge specified by the behavioral rules below. 1. Module and ports



3. Reset

The reset input r is active high and synchronous to the rising edge of clk. Before the first rising edge of clk, q is unknown, regardless of d or r; the implementation must leave q uninitialised, with no initial block or declaration initialiser establishing a value.

At every rising edge for which r is high, q becomes 0 immediately after that edge. This reset action has priority over d. Asserting or releasing r between rising edges does not immediately change q. On the first rising edge after r is released, q captures the value of d present at that edge. The sole stored state is the one-bit value represented by q together with whether that value has yet been established; reset establishes q as 0.

4. Timing

q is a registered output. It changes only immediately after a rising edge of clk and holds its value between rising edges. Its latency is one rising-edge sampling step from d or r under the normative pre-edge trace convention: inputs associated with row k are sampled at that row's rising edge, and the resulting q is first shown in row k+1. There are no pulse outputs or periodic outputs generated by the module.

The evaluated object uses a full-cycle timebase: each successive row represents one complete clock cycle and one rising-edge update, after settling. This observation convention does not impose a 10 ns clock tick. The clock's physical period and propagation delay are not specified, so no numerical nanosecond period or delay requirement is imposed.

5. Behaviour

Only a rising edge of clk updates q, sampling r and d with r taking priority; steady high or low clk and falling edges have no effect, and every repeated rising edge performs a new update without any transaction, interruption, or busy mode.

At every rising edge of clk with r low, q captures the contemporaneous value of d; changes to d between rising edges have no effect on q, d is ignored when r is high, and repeated captures are independent with no interruption or transaction state.

r is an active-high synchronous control: when high at a rising edge of clk it makes q 1'b0 in priority to d, while assertion or release between rising edges has no immediate effect, and each subsequent rising edge independently applies the then-current r value.

q is undefined before the first rising edge, changes only just after a rising edge to 1'b0 when r is sampled high or otherwise to sampled d, holds between rising edges, and under pre-edge row sampling the result of row k's edge is first observable in row k+1; reset release, repeated resets, and successive captures require no completion interval and each new edge immediately determines the next held value.

All ports are one bit, so d and r each have exactly the encodings 0 and 1. At a rising edge, r=1 produces q=0 for either value of d; r=0 produces q=d. No start, enable, busy, completion, cancellation, or transaction state exists. Every rising edge independently replaces the held result, including repeated resets and consecutive captures.
