Event convention: row k drives input i[k], waits for combinational settling and observes out(s[k], i[k]) BEFORE the active clock edge specified by the behavioral rules below. Use those rules for clock polarity and reset sensitivity. That edge produces s[k+1] = step(s[k], i[k]). A register assigned input i[k] at that edge is visible in row k+1; this is ONE register, not an input register plus an output register. Asynchronous reset assertion takes effect without waiting for that edge. 1. Module and ports



3. Reset

The module has no reset and no state. From power-up, including before any edge has occurred, z is defined solely by the simultaneous x and y values and is never unknown due to initialization.

4. Timing

z is combinational. It continuously reflects the simultaneous x and y inputs with zero clock-cycle latency; there is no capture edge, first-result delay, pulse, periodic output, hold interval, completion event, or retained result.

The worked-trace rows are observations of the settled autonomous combinational design. Consecutive rows correspond to the interface's 5 ns sample spacing, so row k is observed at k times 5 ns; this observation convention is not a circuit clock period or propagation-delay law, neither of which is specified.

5. Behaviour

For every input combination, z is 1 exactly when x and y are equal, and z is 0 exactly when they differ.

At all times and without modes or transaction state, x affects z only through equality with y: either level of x makes z 1 exactly when x equals the simultaneous y value, so changes, simultaneous input changes, interruptions, and repeated changes are reflected combinationally with no capture or retention.

At all times and without modes or transaction state, y affects z only through equality with x: either level of y makes z 1 exactly when y equals the simultaneous x value, so changes, simultaneous input changes, interruptions, and repeated changes are reflected combinationally with no capture or retention.

There is no reset or edge-dependent timing: z continuously equals 1 when the current x and y are equal and 0 otherwise, so a pre-edge row observes the values present before that edge, with no first-result latency, capture edge, hold or clear interval, completion, retention, or restart behavior.

Thus the complete truth table is: x=0 and y=0 gives z=1; x=0 and y=1 gives z=0; x=1 and y=0 gives z=0; and x=1 and y=1 gives z=1.
