1. Module and ports



3. Reset

areset is an active-high asynchronous reset. Whenever areset is asserted, out is 1 immediately, in every reachable condition, and reset takes priority over a coincident rising edge. While areset remains asserted, out remains 1. Deasserting areset has no immediate effect. On the first rising edge after release, out remains 1 if in is 1 and becomes 0 if in is 0.

The Moore state denoted by out is reset to the state whose output is 1. Before any reset assertion or rising clk edge has established a state, out is unknown regardless of in. The implementation must leave this power-up state uninitialised: it must not give it a declaration initialiser or an initial-block value.

4. Timing

out is a registered Moore output with zero additional pipeline stages. With areset low, it changes immediately after each rising clk edge according to the value of in sampled at that edge. Changes of in at other times do not affect out. Reset assertion affects out asynchronously; reset deassertion alone does not.

Each rising edge is an independent update opportunity. There are no completion pulses, restart intervals, or periodic output signals.

For the evaluated full-cycle trace, row k is the settled pre-edge observation at k * step_ns using the interface's step_ns and sample_offset_ns convention; the transition associated with that row first appears in the following post-edge observation. This trace-observation spacing is not a specification of the circuit's physical clock period or propagation delay.

5. Behaviour

The complete state/output law is as follows. While areset is high, out is 1 and in has no effect. An assertion of areset coincident with a rising clk edge overrides that edge's ordinary update.

While areset is low, only a rising clk edge updates out: if in is 1, out holds its previous value; if in is 0, out becomes the complement of its previous value. A steady clk level and falling edges have no effect. Repeated rising edges apply this same law independently, including repeated samples of the same input value. Changes to in between rising edges have no effect.

After reset is released, out retains 1 until a later rising clk edge applies the in-dependent update law. Repeated reset assertions have the same behavior. Before a reset assertion or rising edge has established a value, ordinary rising-edge behavior cannot turn an unknown prior value into a known one: sampling either value of in leaves out unknown until reset is asserted.

The public parameters do not alter this one-bit Moore-machine behavior.
