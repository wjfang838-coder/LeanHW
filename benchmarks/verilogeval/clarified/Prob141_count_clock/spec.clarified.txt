<!-- original-requirements:start -->

I would like you to implement a module named TopModule with the following
interface. All input and output ports are one bit unless otherwise
specified.

 - input  clk
 - input  reset
 - input  ena
 - output pm
 - output hh (8 bits)
 - output mm (8 bits)
 - output ss (8 bits)

Create a set of counters suitable for use as a 12-hour clock (with am/pm
indicator). Your counters are clocked by a fast-running clk, with a pulse
on ena whenever your clock should increment (i.e., once per second, while
"clk" is much faster than once per second). The signal "pm" is asserted
if the clock is PM, or is otherwise AM. hh, mm, and ss are two BCD
(Binary- Coded Decimal) digits each for hours (01-12), minutes (00-59),
and seconds (00-59). Reset is the active high synchronous signal that
resets the clock to "12:00 AM." Reset has higher priority than enable and
can occur even when not enabled. Assume all sequential logic is triggered
on the positive edge of the clock.

Decisions and verification instance

Module and integration contract

Module: TopModule.
Ports below are in exact declaration order:
- clk: input 1 bit.
- reset: input 1 bit.
- ena: input 1 bit.
- pm: output 1 bit.
- hh: output [7:0].
- mm: output [7:0].
- ss: output [7:0].

Public parameters:
- None.

Event convention: row k drives input i[k], waits for combinational settling and observes out(s[k], i[k]) BEFORE the active clock edge specified by the behavioral rules below. Use those rules for clock polarity and reset sensitivity. That edge produces s[k+1] = step(s[k], i[k]). A register assigned input i[k] at that edge is visible in row k+1; this is ONE register, not an input register plus an output register. If the behavioral rules specify an asynchronous reset, its assertion takes effect without waiting for that edge; a synchronous reset acts only on its stated edge. Describe transaction latency from the accepting edge, and distinguish the completion pulse from the interval during which result data remains valid.

1. Module and ports


3. Reset
reset is active high and synchronous to the rising edge of clk; every edge sampling reset=1 restores pm=0, hh=BCD 12, mm=BCD 00 and ss=BCD 00 regardless of ena.
These are the values of all stored clock state and every output immediately after that edge. Repeated reset edges retain those values. Assertion and release between rising edges have no effect on state or outputs. While reset is high before its first sampled rising edge, the prior values persist; after a sampled reset edge they are the reset values.

The certain scoped answers Q1-Q4 and Q14-Q17 establish four-state hardware unknowns: before the first rising edge sampling reset=1, pm is entirely X and hh, mm and ss are each entirely X. Edges sampling reset=0 do not resolve those unknowns, regardless of ena. Merely asserting reset does not resolve them. This is an answer-established hardware rule, not an initialization implementation choice or a requirement inferred from model initialization. No reset-before-use input restriction is imposed.

Let E0 be a rising edge sampling reset=1. Immediately before E0 the prior state is observed, including X if no earlier reset edge occurred. Immediately after E0 the time is 12:00:00 AM. Release itself causes no update. E1 is the first subsequent rising edge sampling reset=0: ena=0 holds 12:00:00 AM, while ena=1 produces 12:00:01 AM immediately after E1. If E2 and E3 also sample reset=0 and ena=1, their immediate results are 12:00:02 AM and 12:00:03 AM. A disabled edge retains the preceding time; any edge sampling reset=1 restarts this timeline.

4. Timing
clk acts only on its rising edge; falling edges and intervals at either steady clock level retain all outputs even when reset or ena changes.
All four outputs are registered. Each rising edge computes their post-edge values from the pre-edge time and sampled reset and ena. The result is visible immediately after that same edge, with no additional publication edge or pipeline delay. Outputs retain their values between rising edges. There is no output completion pulse, bounded transaction or terminal halt.

The finite model uses full-cycle, active-edge scenarios: row k observes the settled state before its associated rising edge, and that row's inputs determine the transition first observed at row k+1. clk is implicit in this projection. A row with reset=1 represents assertion held to its associated rising edge. Between-edge changes are not simulated by the table; their no-effect law remains part of the circuit contract. iface.json supplies neither step_ns nor sample_offset_ns, so no nanosecond observation spacing is asserted.

The fast-running clk period is externally supplied, with no numeric period specified. ena is intended to pulse once per second. It is a level sampled at each rising edge, and its duration is not restricted to one cycle. With ena continuously high and reset low after initialization, the ss sequence repeats every 60 cycles, the mm sequence every 3600 cycles, and the hh sequence every 43200 cycles. pm has a period of 86400 cycles, comprising 43200 cycles low and 43200 cycles high. Disabling counting extends these durations by the disabled cycles; these are enabled-edge counts, not an autonomous frequency guarantee.

5. Behaviour
pm=0 indicates AM and pm=1 indicates PM. Each eight-bit time output consists of an unsigned BCD tens digit in bits 7:4 and units digit in bits 3:0. hh covers 01 through 12; mm and ss cover 00 through 59. BCD(n) means 16*floor(n/10)+(n modulo 10).

ena is sampled at every rising edge: with reset=0, ena=0 preserves all outputs and ena=1 advances initialized time by exactly one second, including on every edge of a sustained high ena.
reset overrides both ena levels. Disabled edges pause indefinitely; subsequent enabled edges continue from retained time. Every later reset edge interrupts counting and restores 12:00 AM. There is no completion condition or ignored repeated enable. All binary combinations of reset and ena are covered; neither input is an edge-triggered request signal.

With reset=0 and ena=1, ss advances in BCD through 00,...,09,10,...,59,00, with 59 becoming 00 at one edge and no intermediate observable value.
With reset=0 and ena=1, mm advances in BCD through 00,...,09,10,...,59,00 exactly when pre-edge ss=BCD 59, and otherwise holds.
With reset=0 and ena=1, hh advances in BCD through 01,...,09,10,11,12,01 exactly when pre-edge mm and ss both equal BCD 59, and otherwise holds.
With reset=0 and ena=1, pm toggles exactly when pre-edge hh=BCD 11 and mm=ss=BCD 59, and otherwise holds in initialized operation.

All four new values are published simultaneously from the old time. In particular, the same-edge change of ss to 00 does not inhibit the carry into mm, and the same-edge change of mm to 00 does not inhibit the carry into hh. Advancing 11:59:59 to 12:00:00 toggles pm; advancing 12:59:59 to 01:00:00 preserves pm. Decimal digit carry is simultaneous, so 09 advances to 10 without an intermediate digit value. Counting remains within the stated BCD ranges for every initialized reachable state. Before initialization the explicitly authorized X law applies, rather than a fabricated invalid-BCD input operation; the time outputs are not data inputs.

After a reset edge E0 followed by n enabled edges without another reset, ss=BCD(n modulo 60), mm=BCD(floor(n/60) modulo 60), and the hours follow the stated 12-hour cycle from 12. Disabled edges do not contribute to n. All conditions above refer to pre-edge operands; registered post-edge results do not depend on an already advanced output.
